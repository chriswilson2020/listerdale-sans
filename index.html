Designed for reading

Some fonts add avoidable visual ambiguity to reading. Listerdale Sans distinguishes commonly confused characters, keeps punctuation visible and gives words slightly more space — without looking like a specialist typeface.

1lI

One, lowercase l and capital I each take a different shape - the I carries top and bottom crossbars - so codes and references read without guesswork.

quip

The q carries a tail on its descender, so p and q are no longer mirror twins.

wow

An open, uncrossed w. One less unfamiliar shape to decode.

bd.

The d runs narrower than the b, quietly adding another cue beyond direction alone. Full stops and commas are enlarged so punctuation remains visible, and the lowercase runs slightly taller for easier recognition at small sizes.

0123456789

Lining figures align steadily in tables, and the zero carries a centre dot so it never reads as O.

room to breathe and read
room to breathe and read

Built-in spacing is set against unmodified Raleway as the baseline: letter sidebearings are increased by 12 units each — 2.4% of an em in total — and the word space (255 to 325 units) is 27% wider than Raleway's default. Both figures are measurable from the two font files. Add more with CSS or your app's settings; the font won't fight you.

grounded

Stroke weight settles gently toward the baseline, anchoring each letter. An intentional design feature of the text cut, not an established reading intervention.

Text and Display

Same letterforms, same widths, two finishes. The text cut grades stroke weight gently toward the baseline for comfortable reading; Display keeps strokes crisp and even for large sizes. Swapping between them never reflows a line.

Grounded

Text cut - body copy and everything you read. Look at the stroke bottoms: slightly heavier, anchoring each letter.

Crisp

Display - headings, titles and anything above about 20pt. Even strokes, clean at any scale. This page's own headings are set in it.

Why we made it

Our work runs on documents: proposals, reports, slide decks. If one reader in ten finds them harder to read than they should be, that is a communication problem, and we sit inside it. We wanted a typeface that removed the friction without looking like an aid. The ones that existed either announced themselves loudly or came with licence fees, so we drew our own and decided it should be free. If it makes one report easier for someone to read, releasing it was worth the effort.
